# FLL Challenge Judging Rubric — BioGlow Season (2026)

> **PRIORITY: This document is the source of truth for everything the team does.**
> Every process, daily log entry, and dashboard feature in this program should trace back to a row in this rubric. If an activity doesn't help us move a rubric row from *Beginning* toward *Exceeds*, we should question why we're doing it.

Source: `fll-challenge-bioglow-rubrics-color.pdf` (official FIRST LEGO League Challenge rubrics, color version).

---

## How Judging Works

- Teams communicate their achievement to the judges in each criterion below. The **Innovation Project rubric** is filled out from the Innovation Project presentation; the **Robot Design rubric** is filled out from the Robot Design explanation.
- Judges are **required** to tick one box on each row to indicate the level the team achieved.
- If the team **EXCEEDS** (level 4), the judge must write a short comment explaining *how* the team exceeded.
- **Core Values are the lens through which judges watch everything.** There is no separate Core Values rubric — rows marked ⚙️ below count **dually** toward that rubric's award *and* the Core Values award rankings.
- **Core Values make up 25% of a team's Champion's score** and should be demonstrated throughout the team's work and the entire event experience.

### The Four Achievement Levels

| Level | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Beginning** | Minimal / unclear evidence |
| 2 | **Developing** | Partial / simple evidence |
| 3 | **Accomplished** | Clear / detailed evidence |
| 4 | **Exceeds** | Beyond Accomplished — judge must write how |

> **Team goal:** "Accomplished" (3) on every row is the baseline target. "Exceeds" (4) requires giving the judges something memorable enough to write a comment about.

## Innovation Project Rubric

*"Teams should communicate to the judges their achievement in each of the following criteria."*

⚙️ = row also counts toward Core Values.

### IDENTIFY — Team had a clearly defined problem that was well researched.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem definition | Unclear definition of the problem | Partially clear definition of the problem | **Clear definition of the problem** |
| ⚙️ Research | Minimal evidence of research | Partial evidence of research from one or more sources | **Clear, detailed research from a variety of sources** |

### DESIGN — Team worked together while creating a project plan and developing their ideas.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project plan | Minimal evidence of an effective project plan | Partial evidence of an effective project plan | **Clear evidence of an effective project plan** |
| ⚙️ Team involvement | Minimal evidence that development process involved all team members | Partial evidence that development process involved all team members | **Clear evidence that development process involved all team members** |

### CREATE — Team developed an original idea or built on an existing one with a prototype model/drawing to represent their solution.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⚙️ Innovation | Minimal explanation of innovation in solution | Simple explanation of innovation in solution | **Detailed explanation of innovation in solution** |
| Model/drawing | Unclear model/drawing that represents the solution | Simple model/drawing that represents the solution | **Detailed model/drawing that represents the solution** |

### ITERATE — Team shared their ideas with others, collected feedback, and included improvements to their solution.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sharing | Minimal sharing of their solution with others | Solution shared with at least one person/group | **Solution shared with multiple people/groups** |
| Improvements | Minimal evidence of improvements based on feedback | Partial evidence of improvements based on feedback | **Clear evidence of improvements based on feedback** |

### COMMUNICATE — Team shared an effective presentation of their solution, its impact on others, and celebrated their team's progress.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⚙️ Explanation & impact | Unclear explanation of the solution and its potential impact on others | Partially clear explanation of solution and its potential impact on others | **Clear explanation of solution and its potential impact on others** |
| ⚙️ Pride & enthusiasm | Presentation shows minimal pride or enthusiasm for their work | Presentation shows partial pride or enthusiasm for their work | **Presentation clearly shows pride or enthusiasm for their work** |

## Robot Design Rubric

*"Teams should communicate to the judges their achievement in each of the following criteria."*

⚙️ = row also counts toward Core Values.

### IDENTIFY — Team determined which missions to attempt, explored building and coding resources, and sought guidance as needed.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mission strategy | Minimal evidence of mission strategy | Partial evidence of mission strategy | **Clear evidence of mission strategy** |
| ⚙️ Use of resources | Minimal use of building or coding resources | Some use of building or coding resources | **Clear use of building or coding resources to support their mission strategy** |

### DESIGN — Team members worked collaboratively on their designs and developed the building and coding skills needed.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⚙️ All members contributed | Minimal evidence that all team members contributed ideas | Partial evidence that all team members contributed ideas | **Clear evidence that all team members contributed ideas** |
| Skills in all members | Minimal evidence of building and coding skills in all team members | Partial evidence of building and coding skills in all team members | **Clear evidence of building and coding skills in all team members** |

### CREATE — Team developed original designs or improved on existing ones according to their mission strategy.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attachments | Unclear explanation of attachments and their purpose | Simple explanation of attachments and their purpose | **Clear explanation of innovative attachments and their purpose** |
| Code / sensors | Unclear explanation of code and/or sensor use | Simple explanation of code and/or sensor use | **Clear explanation of innovative code and/or sensor use** |

### ITERATE — Team repeatedly tested their robot and code to identify areas for improvement and incorporated the findings into their solutions.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Testing | Minimal evidence of testing their robot and code | Partial evidence of testing their robot and code | **Clear evidence of repeated testing of their robot and code** |
| ⚙️ Improvements | Minimal evidence of improvements based on testing | Partial evidence of improvements based on testing | **Clear evidence of improvements based on testing** |

### COMMUNICATE — Team effectively explained what they learned from the robot design process and celebrated their progress.

| Row | Beginning (1) | Developing (2) | Accomplished (3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⚙️ Process & lessons | Unclear explanation of process and lessons learned | Simple explanation of process and lessons learned | **Detailed explanation of process and lessons learned** |
| ⚙️ Pride & enthusiasm | Team shows minimal pride or enthusiasm for their work | Team shows partial pride or enthusiasm for their work | **Team clearly shows pride or enthusiasm for their work** |

## Judging Session Feedback Sheet

Used by judges to record written feedback after the Innovation Project presentation and Robot Design explanation. Returned to teams at the end of the event. Two columns per section: **"Great job..."** and **"Think about..."**

| Section | Judges' guiding question |
|---|---|
| **Core Values** | How did the team demonstrate **teamwork, discovery, inclusion, innovation, impact, and fun** in their work? |
| **Innovation Project** | How did the team identify and approach solving a problem connected to the season theme? |
| **Robot Design** | How did the team approach solving robot game missions using building and coding? |

### Optional Judged Awards

| Award | Given to |
|---|---|
| **Breakthrough Award** | A team that made significant progress in their confidence and capability in at least one of the core areas of FIRST LEGO League |
| **Rising All-Star** | A team that the judges notice and expect great things from in the future |
| **Motivate** | A team that embraces the culture of FIRST LEGO League through team building, team spirit, and enthusiasm |

## What the Rubric Tells Us (implications for our program)

1. **Both rubrics share the same 5-phase skeleton: IDENTIFY → DESIGN → CREATE → ITERATE → COMMUNICATE.** Our Innovation Project process and Robot Design process should be organized around these exact five phases so evidence maps 1:1 to rubric rows.
2. **"Evidence" is the recurring word.** Judges score what they can *see*. The daily log / engineering notebook exists to generate clear, dated evidence for every row — that's its entire job.
3. **"All team members" appears twice in Robot Design and once in Innovation Project.** The dashboard must track *who did what* (attachments, missions, code, research, prototype) so we can prove full participation.
4. **Iteration is scored in both rubrics.** Every test, every piece of feedback, and every improvement needs to be logged with a before → after trail.
5. **Core Values are woven through 11 of the 20 rubric rows (⚙️) and are 25% of the Champion's score.** They are not a side quest — the daily log should capture a Core Values observation at every meeting.
6. **Pride and enthusiasm are literally scored.** Celebrating progress (and recording those celebrations) is a rubric strategy, not just morale.
